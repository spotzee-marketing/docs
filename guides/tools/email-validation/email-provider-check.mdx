Use the email provider check to look for a recognised mail host in a domain's MX records. Provider recognition helps you inspect routing; it does not establish that a particular mailbox exists or accepts mail.

<Info>
Open the [email provider check](https://spotzee.com/tools/email-provider-check/). Enter a domain or full email address. Extended API calls use your Spotzee credit balance; see [pricing](https://spotzee.com/pricing/) for current rates.
</Info>

## Look up a provider

<Steps>
  <Step title="Enter the domain or address">
    For API access, submit `email_domain` to the email provider check endpoint. When you provide an address, the part after `@` is used as the domain.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Inspect a recognised match">
    A recognised provider returns `data.exists: true`, `data.emailProvider` and `data.mxRecord`. The lookup checks the first returned MX record; it does not sort the records to select the lowest preference value.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Investigate an unrecognised result">
    `data.exists: false` can mean that no provider pattern matched, even when MX records exist. It does not prove the domain cannot receive email. Use [MX lookup](/guides/tools/dns-auth/mx-lookup) to inspect the records directly.
  </Step>
</Steps>

An MX record can point to a gateway or forwarding service rather than the final mailbox host. Treat the detected name as a routing clue. Do not remove contacts solely because a provider was not recognised.

A provider check concerns incoming mail routing. It does not identify which platform sent a marketing message. For address assessment, combine [syntax validation](/guides/tools/email-validation/email-syntax-validator), the [temporary email check](/guides/tools/email-validation/temp-email-checker) and [email validation](/guides/tools/email-validation/email-validation) according to your task.
