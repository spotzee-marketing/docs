Use the readability scorer to compare the reading complexity of text as you revise it. It calculates several formula scores and an estimated reading time. These are text measures, not evidence that a particular audience will read, understand, or act on the message.

<Info>
Try the scorer at [spotzee.com/tools/readability-scorer](https://spotzee.com/tools/readability-scorer).
</Info>

## Metrics returned

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `fleschReadingEase` | Reading Ease score; higher usually indicates simpler text. |
| `fleschKincaidGrade` | Flesch-Kincaid grade estimate. |
| `smogIndex` | Estimate based on polysyllabic words; returns zero for fewer than three detected sentences. |
| `colemanLiauIndex` | Grade estimate based on characters and sentences. |
| `automatedReadabilityIndex` | Grade estimate based on characters, words, and sentences. |
| `linsearWriteFormula` | Grade estimate using a sample of up to 100 words. |
| `rix` | Long words per detected sentence. |
| `medianGrade` | Median of grade estimates derived from the formulas. |
| `readingTime` | Estimated seconds at about 4.17 words per second. |

The formulas use approximate English word, syllable, and sentence detection. Most metrics sample up to the first 1,000 words; `readingTime` uses the full text. Results from short fragments, unusual punctuation, or other languages need particular care.

## Check and revise text

<Steps>
  <Step title="Enter your text">
    Paste the paragraph or message you want to assess.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Compare the metrics">
    Look at Reading Ease alongside the grade estimates. A difference between formulas is normal because they measure different features.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Revise and check again">
    Shorten sentences or replace complex wording where that improves clarity for your readers, then compare the new result.
  </Step>
</Steps>
