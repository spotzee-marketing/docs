## Why this matters

Writing one subject line is easy. Writing three or four strong variants for an A/B test — each different enough to generate useful data — is harder. A subject line generator removes the blank-page problem. It gives you a pool of alternatives in seconds, so you spend your time selecting and refining rather than drafting from nothing.

Generated suggestions also surface phrasings you might not reach on your own. The AI varies tone, personalisation, urgency framing and emoji use across suggestions, which means the pool often includes at least one option that reads differently enough to test meaningfully against your original. And when a subject line scorer flags your draft as too spammy, a generator gives you somewhere to go next — alternatives built to avoid the same triggers.

## What to watch for

- **Score each suggestion before using it.** Generated subject lines are not guaranteed to be spam-free. Run each one you are considering through the [subject line scorer](/guides/tools/content-analysis/email-subject-score) to confirm it clears the main signals.
- **Check personalisation tokens match your platform.** Suggestions may include `[FNAME]` tokens. Confirm your email service provider uses the same merge tag format, or replace `[FNAME]` with your platform's equivalent (e.g. `{{first_name}}`).
- **Verify emoji rendering across clients.** Some suggestions include emojis. Check that they render correctly in your primary send clients — certain Unicode ranges are stripped or misrendered by some email clients.
- **Review for brand voice.** AI suggestions match the intent of your draft but may not match your brand tone. Review each suggestion before adding it to a campaign.
